Abstract 22 
Background and aims : Emerging evidence highlight the gut microbiome as a n 23 
important regulator of metabolic health, with probiotics and prebiotics demonstrating 24 
exciting potential  for their role in health promotion . This study aims to  investigate  a 25 
novel synbiotic formulation  comprising four probiotic strains ( Bifidobacterium 26 
animalis subsp. lactis CECT 8145, and three Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus  strains), 27 
prebiotics (inulin, fructooligosaccharides), and Chrysanthemum morifolium  extract. 28 
We hypothesized that this intervention  would improve metabolic health parameters, 29 
particularly visceral adiposity.   30 
Methods: In a  12-week, double -blind, randomized, placebo -controlled, parallel -31 
group trial with a 6-week post-intervention follow -up, 112 participants  (BMI: 24.0–32 
34.9 kg/m²) received daily synbiotic or a matched placebo. Changes in visceral 33 
adipose tissue (VAT) area, serving as the primary endpoint, were quantified by dual -34 
energy X -ray absorptiometry (DXA). Secondary outcomes included analysis of 35 
blood biochemical parameters, body composition , and fecal microbiota 36 
characterization.   37 
Results: Compared with placebo, synbiotic supplementation significantly reduced 38 
VAT area from baseline to week 12 (p = 0.048). In subgroup analyses by gender and 39 
BMI, the ef fect was more pronounced in men than in women (p = 0.051) and was 40 
highly significant in individuals with 24 ≤ BMI < 28 (p = 0.003).  However, 41 
subcutaneous adipose tissue (SAT) increased in the 24 ≤ BMI < 28 subgroup  (p = 42 
0.027). Although no significant changes occurred in blood biochemistry, BMI, or 43

1. Introduction  56 
Overweight and obesity , characterized by abnormal or excessive fat accumulation, 57 
represent a pressing global health crisis with heterogeneous diagnostic criteria across 58 
populations . Asian populations demonstrate distinct BMI thresholds, with China 59 
defining overweight as BMI ≥24 kg/m² and obesity as BMI ≥28 kg/m², while 60 
Japan/Korea employ lower cutoffs (≥23 kg/m² and ≥25 kg/m², respectively). This 61 
contrasts with the World Health Organization’s (WHO) definition , where overweight  62 
is defined as having a BMI ≥25 kg/m², and obesity: ≥30 kg/m². Particularly 63 
concerning is abdominal obesity (waist circumference ≥90 cm [men]/ ≥85 cm 64 
[women]), which exhibits a criteria strongly correlated to  metabolic dysregulation  65

[1]. The global obesity pandemic affected over 2.5 billion adults in 2016, with 66 
escalating prevalence driving increased incidence of obesity -associated 67 
comorbidities , including insulin resistance, type 2 diab etes (T2D),  metabolic 68 
dysfunction -associated steatotic liver disease  (MASLD), and cardiovascular diseases  69 
(CVD) [2, 3]. Given the escalating burden of obesity -related health issues, there is 70 
an urgent need for effective interventions to address this global epidemic.  71 
Emerging research implicates gut microbiota dysbiosis in obesity pathogenesis 72 
through modulat ion of energy harvest, central appetite  regulation , adipogenesis , 73 
systemic inflammation, and circadian metabolic regulation [4]. Specific probiotic 74 
genera (e.g.,  Lactobacillus , Bifidobacterium , Akkermansia ) have been shown to 75 
demonstrate anti -obesity potential, positioning microbiota modulation as a novel 76 
strategy for health promotion [4, 5]. Multiple narrative reviews as well as systematic 77 
reviews and meta -analyses substantiate that probiotic/prebiotic/synbiotic 78 
interventions may beneficially influence body composi tion and cardiometabolic 79 
markers in overweight populations  [6-10]. These findings highlight the potential of 80 
microbiota-targeted interventions as a novel approach to obesity management.   81 
Notably, Bifidobacterium animalis  subsp. lactis CECT 8145, isolated from the gut of 82 
breastfed infants,  exhibits validated anti -adipogenic effects across multiple 83 
experimental models, including  Caenorhabditis elegans , rodent systems, and human 84 
clinical trials  [11-13]. While Lactobacillus rhamnosus  strains demonstrate intestinal 85 
epithelial adhesion capacity and enteropathogen inhibition [14, 15]. Current evidence 86 
suggests multi -strain formulations may yield superior efficacy to monotherapies  [16], 87

2.2. Synbiotic product 135 
Both the synbiotic product (Nutrilite Body Lift) and the placebo were supplied 136 
by Nutrilite, Amway, in identical containers labeled with numeric codes to maintain 137 
blinding. The active product was a beverage mix in single -serving stick pouches, 138 
each containing 2 g of powder and with a shelf life of 24 months under recommended 139 
storage conditions. At the time of manufacture, each stick delivered 6.0 × 10 ¹⁰ 140 
colony-forming units ( CFU) of viable probiotics, with a guaranteed minimum of ≥141 
 4.5 × 10¹⁰ CFU maintained through the end of shelf life. Key functional ingredients 142 
per stick included  1.5×1010 CFU total of 4 viable probiotic strains ( Bifidobacterium 143 
animalis subsp. lactis CECT 8145, Lactobacillus rhamnosus  CECT 8631, L. 144 
rhamnosus 30320, and L. rhamnosus  CNCMI-4036); Inulin; Fructooligosaccharides 145 
(FOS); Chrysanthemum flower  extract. The placebo was formulated to be sensorially 146 
indistinguishable from the active product, mat ching its appearance, flavor, texture, 147 
and packaging. Each 2 g placebo stick contained the same adjuvants but lacked the 148 
probiotic strains, prebiotics, and Chrysanthemum flower  extract. Participants were 149 
instructed to consume one stick daily for 12 weeks. A stability test conducted 30 days 150 
after the last participant ’s visit confirmed that the product retained > 0.7 × 10 ⁹ CFU 151 
per stick at the end of its 24 -month shelf life, meeting regulatory requirements for 152 
probiotic viability.  153

2.3. Experiment design 154 
This study was a single -center, double -blind, randomized, placebo -controlled 155 
trial with equal allocation of participants into two parallel groups. The trial was 156 
conducted at The Second Affiliated Hospital of Zhejiang Chinese Medical University 157 
in Hangzhou, Chin a. Participants who met the inclusion criteria provided written 158 
informed consent and were compensated with transportation subsidies in accordance 159 
with the regulations of the Ethics Committee.  160 
The study comprised two intervention arms : ( 1) CON  group: Partic ipants 161 
received 2 g/ stick/day of a placebo ; (2) SYN group: Participants received 2 162 
g/stick/day of the  synbiotic product  Nutrilite Body Lift . After initial screening and 163 
baseline assessments, participants were instructed to consume the assigned study 164 
product daily for a duration of 12 weeks. Clinic visits were scheduled at 6 and 12 165 
weeks of the intervention period to monitor progress and assess outcomes. 166 
Additionally, periodic follow -ups were conducted by research personnel  via WeChat 167 
or telephone to ensure compliance and to identify any adverse events. Six weeks after 168 
the conclusion of the intervention, participants attended a final follow -up visit to 169 
evaluate long -term effects . 170 
The primary outcome measure was the change in the abdominal VAT area at 171 
week 12. Secondary outcomes included changes in body composition (body weight, 172 
fat mass, BMI, and subcutaneous adipose tissue [SAT]), waist circumference, blood 173 
parameters (lipid profile, glucose levels, liver function, and inflammatory markers), 174 
and gut microbiot a composition. The safety of the synbiotic product was assessed 175

The relationship between body weight and the gut microbiota has emerged as a 543 
prominent area of interest in metabolic health research, with compelling evidence 544 
linking microbial dysbiosis to obesity pathogenesis  [28]. Probiotic interventions have 545 
shown particular promise in modulating glucose homeostasis, lipid metabolism, and 546 
adiposity distribution, including V AT reduction and hepatic steatosis improvement [29]. 547 
In the present study, the synbiotic intervention resulted in a notable V AT reduction 548 
alongside a non-significant trend of SAT accumulation compared to the placebo control. 549

These contrasting effects on fat distribution were maintained throughout the 6 -week 550

V AT reduction throughout the 18-week intervention period, while female counterparts 553

4.1. Reverse changes of VAT and SAT  560 
The two principal white adipose tissue (WAT) depots - SAT and VAT - exhibit 561 
distinct anatomical and functional characteristics. VAT, comprising omental, 562 
mesenteric, retroperitoneal, gonadal, and pericardial depots, demonstrates strong 563 
associations with obesity -related metabolic pathologies , including insulin resistance, 564 
T2D, MASLD, and CVD [30]. In contrast, SAT serves as a metabolically protective 565 
energy reservoir. Our investigation revealed a clinically significant inverse relationship 566 
between V AT reduction and SAT expansion, with pronounced sexual dimorphism in 567 
these responses. 568 
Emerging evidence suggests that W AT retains the capacity to expand in adulthood 569 
through hypertrophy and/or hyperplasia, particularly in response to energy excess [31]. 570 
V AT, primarily represented by the omental depot, is metabolically active and 571 
Journal Pre-proof


--- PAGE 29 ---
 27 
contributes to systemic inflammation and insulin resistance. In contrast, SAT, found in 572 
the abdominal, gluteal, and femoral regions, serves as a protective buffer, preventing 573 
ectopic lipid deposition and mitigating metabolic dysfunction. The inability of the SAT 574 
to expand can lead to lipotoxicity, insulin resistance, and obesity-related complications 575 
[32].  576 
The observed expansion of SAT in our study may reflect a protective mechanism, 577 
redirecting lipid storage away from visceral depots. This process is likely mediated by 578 
the proliferation and differentiation of preadipocytes into mature adipocytes, which is 579 
more pronounced in SAT than in V AT  [33]. Similar mechanisms have been observed 580 
with thiazolidinediones, a class of anti -diabetic drugs that promote fat redistribution 581 
from V AT to SAT by activating peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor (PPAR)γ, a 582 
key regulator of adipocyte differentiation [34-36]. Recent studies further highlight the 583 
metabolic benefits of S AT. For instance, large -scale SAT liposuction in humans has 584 
been shown to confer negligible metabolic benefits, while mouse models demonstrate 585 
that SAT transplantation into the visceral cavity improves insulin sensitivity and 586 
reduces body weight and fat ma ss [37]. These findings emphasize the fundamental 587 
metabolic distinctions between SAT and V AT and the protectiv e role of SAT in 588 
maintaining metabolic homeostasis. 589 
The significant reduction in V AT observed in the overall SYN group (-4.49 cm²; p 590 
= 0.048) and particularly in the subgroup with 24 ≤ BMI < 28 (-6.28 cm²; p = 0.003) is 591 
consistent with findings from prior probiotic studies in overweight and obese 592 
populations. A recent meta-analysis of 14 trials involving 1,523 participants reported a 593 
Journal Pre-proof


--- PAGE 30 ---
 28 
pooled V AT reduction of -3.63 cm² (95% CI: −5.08 to −2.17; p < 0.001) following 594 
probiotic interventions [38]. Our results exceed this average, suggesting a potentially 595 
stronger metabolic effect of the synbiotic regimen compared to conventional probiotic 596 
therapies. Notably, unlike dietary or exercise -based interventions, our synbiot ic 597 
approach did not lead to significant reductions in total body weight. This indicates that 598 
the observed V AT changes may reflect localized fat redistribution rather than systemic 599 
weight loss—a phenomenon potentially influenced by seasonal variations. For instance, 600 
it has been reported that individuals tend to accumulate SAT during colder months, 601 
possibly as an adaptive response to cold exposure [39]. Consistent with this, all 602 
treatment groups in our study showed an increase in SAT. Furthermore, V AT area is 603 
generally larger in individuals sampled from winter to spring compared to those 604 
sampled in summer and a utumn [39], which may be attributed to reduced physical 605 
activity and/or increased caloric intake in cold er seasons. Supporting this notion, 606 
exercise in cool environments has been shown to preserve subcutaneous fat, whereas 607 
similar exercise under thermoneutral conditions reduces it [40]. Although overall 608 
weight loss was minimal, it is important to emphasize that V AT is a more robust 609 
predictor of cardiometabolic risk than general adiposity [41, 42] . The absence of 610 
significant weight reduction in our study may indicate that sustained V AT benefits 611 
require either longer intervention periods (e.g., 6–12 months) or timing across warmer 612 
seasons to achieve durable metabolic improvements. The pronounced V AT reduction in 613 
the 24 ≤ BMI < 28 subgroup is promising, though larger and longer -term studies are 614 
necessary to determine whether these changes translate into meaning ful clinical 615

outcomes, such as reduced incidence of type 2 diabetes or cardiovascular disease. 616 
In our trial, 12 weeks of synbiotic supplementation produced a marked and 617

of effect.  Nevertheless, it remains unclear whether such benefits extend beyond this 620

4.2. Sex and BMI differences in fat redistribution  652 
The well -established sexual dimorphism in adipose tissue distribution, 653 
characterized by greater central adiposity in men versus peripheral fat deposition in 654 
women, appears fundamentally linked to sex hormone regulation [45]. Estrogens, for 655 
instance, promote adipose tissue deposition and function, either directly or through 656 
receptor activation in adipocytes and adipose tissues. Estrogens are not only beneficial 657 
to women’s health but also essential for men. Research suggests that estrogens may 658 
selectively increase sympathetic tone in different adipose tissue depots, leading to lipid 659

demonstrating synbiotic -induced V AT reduction in males concurrent with SAT 664 
expansion in females . These sex-specific responses may be attributed to hormonal 665 
influences on adipose tissue dynamics. Estrogens may enhance the capacity of SAT to 666 
expand in women, while androgens in men may promote visceral fat accumulation and 667 
its subsequent reduction in response to intervention. 668 
Further stratification by BMI revealed additional metabolic nuances, where 669 
participants with 24 ≤ BMI < 28 exhibited significant V AT reduction, SAT expansion, 670 
and elevated BMR, contrasting with the null response in obese (BMI ≥28) individuals. 671 
This discrepancy may be explained by differences in metabolic status and adipose tissue 672

Preadipocytes in SAT from lean individuals exhibit higher expression of pro-adipogenic 675

5. Conclusions  836 
This 12-week randomized controlled trial  revealed that synbiotic intervention 837 
induces clinically meaningful reductions in  abdominal V AT among overweight and 838 
obese Chinese adults, with particularly pronounced effects in men and individuals with 839 
a 24 ≤ BMI < 28. The probiotic strains Bifidobacterium animalis  subsp. lactis and 840 
Lactobacillus rhamnosus, key components of the synbiotic, were notably enriched in 841 
the gut microbiota by week 12, though their presence did not persist post-intervention. 842 
Sex-specific effects were prominent: men experienced a significant reduction in V AT at 843 
week 12, alongside improved α-diversity and distinct changes in the β-diversity of the 844 
gut microbiota. In contrast, women showed no significant changes in V AT or gut 845 
microbiota diversity. Subgroup analysis further highlighted that participant s with a 24 846 
≤ BMI < 28 in the synbiotic group demonstrated a significant decrease in V AT, an 847 
increase in SAT, and a higher BMR. These findings emphasize the potential of 848 
synbiotics for t argeted abdominal fat reduction and gut microbiota modulation, with 849 
effects varying by sex and body weight. Future research should investigate the 850 
underlying mechanisms and optimize synbiotic interventions for diverse populations. 851
